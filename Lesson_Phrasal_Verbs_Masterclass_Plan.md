## 🧠 Part 0: Teacher Cheat-Sheet (Why Diego Struggles & How to Explain It)

### 1. Why Spanish Speakers Hate Phrasal Verbs (The "Two-Layer" Secret)
English has two vocabulary layers:
- **Layer 1 — Formal Latin Verbs:** *deploy, resolve, indicate, approve, postpone, investigate, reduce.* Diego already knows these effortlessly because they are almost identical to Spanish (*desplegar, resolver, indicar, aprobar, posponer, investigar, reducir*).
- **Layer 2 — Germanic Phrasal Verbs:** *roll out, iron out, flag up, sign off on, put off, look into, cut down on.* Native speakers (and Dutch colleagues in Amsterdam) use these **80% of the time in spoken meetings and Slack**, because speaking only in Latin verbs sounds like a legal contract.

### 2. Why Traditional Teaching Fails
In Spain, schools teach phrasal verbs grouped by the **verb** (*get up, get in, get over, get by*). That feels 100% random because `get`, `take`, or `put` are empty verbs.
**The secret:** Group them by the **PARTICLE** (`out`, `up`, `off`, `down`) + **Visual Metaphor**. The particle carries 70% of the meaning!

### 3. The 4 Particle Blueprints (Your Core Teaching Framework)
1. **`OUT` = Expanding outward OR Eliminating a problem completely**
   - **`roll out`** *(imagine unrolling a roll of carpet across a floor)* → deploy gradually.
   - **`iron out`** *(imagine running a hot iron over a wrinkled shirt until flat)* → smooth out small bugs/problems.
   - **`rule out`** *(imagine drawing a line with a ruler to cross an option off a list)* → eliminate a possibility (e.g., *"We ruled out a database issue"*).
   - **`phase out`** → gradually retire an old system/legacy code.
2. **`UP` = Making visible / Appearing OR Increasing**
   - **`flag up` / `bring up`** *(imagine raising a red flag in the air so everyone sees it)* → raise a risk or topic in a meeting.
   - **`spin up`** *(imagine a hard drive/engine spinning to life)* → launch a new server, container, or environment quickly.
   - **`scale up`** → increase capacity/resources.
3. **`OFF` = Launching / Separating / Finishing a checkpoint**
   - **`kick off`** *(like kicking the ball at the start of a football match)* → start a meeting or project.
   - **`sign off on`** *(sign a paper and send it off your desk)* → officially approve.
   - **`put off`** *(push something off your desk to a later day)* → postpone/delay.
4. **`DOWN` = Reducing / Breaking into smaller pieces / Crashing**
   - **`break down`** *(smash a big rock into small pieces)* → split a big project into small tickets (or a system failing).
   - **`narrow down`** *(funnel 10 suspects down to 2)* → reduce possibilities during debugging.
   - **`turn down`** *(thumbs down)* → reject an offer or proposal.

### 4. The 1 Grammar Rule He Needs: "The Pronoun Sandwich"
- With a noun, you can put it in the middle or at the end:
  - *"Roll out **the update**"* ✅ OR *"Roll **the update** out"* ✅
- With a pronoun (`it`, `them`), it **MUST go in the middle**:
  - *"We'll **roll it out** tomorrow."* ✅ (NEVER *"roll out it"* ❌)
  - *"Let's **iron them out** first."* ✅ (NEVER *"iron out them"* ❌)
- *Exception:* 3-word phrasal verbs (`sign off on`, `cut down on`) and `look into` cannot be split → *"sign off on **it**"* ✅.

## ⏱️ Minute-by-Minute Lesson Plan (50 Mins)

### 00–07 min | Step 1: Warm-Up + The Nov 13 Quick Fixes
*Paste **Canvas Block 1**.*
1. **Direct Hook (1 min):** *"Diego, in our last lesson you asked a brilliant question: 'Is there an easier way to understand phrasal verbs, or is it impossible?' Today I'm going to show you the system native speakers actually use so you don't have to memorize random lists."*
2. **5-Item Quick Polish from Nov 13 (6 mins):** Have Diego fix his 5 real quotes from Nov 13 out loud:
   - **1:** *"In Amsterdam, we **usually** split the **bill**."* (`usually` = present habit; `used to` = past only).
   - **2:** *"I **was half an hour late** to a **half-hour** meeting."* (no plural on `half-hour`).
   - **3:** *"Is there an easier way to **understand / retain** them?"* (invert `Is there...` for questions).
   - **4:** *"I know **many / a lot of** people at C1 level..."* (`many` for countable people, not `much`).
   - **5:** *"**How long** do you think it'll take before people leak it?"* (`how long` instead of `how much time`).

---

### 07–20 min | Step 2: The Particle Decoder (OUT, UP, OFF, DOWN)
*Paste **Canvas Block 2**.*
- **How to teach it (Interactive, not a lecture!):**
  1. Explain the **Spanish vs. English trap** in 60 seconds: *"In Spanish, you already know English's formal verbs—deploy, resolve, postpone, eliminate—because they're Latin. Phrasal verbs feel random only if you study them by the verb. Instead, look at the second word—the particle."*
  2. Go through the 4 particle groups (`OUT`, `UP`, `OFF`, `DOWN`) on Canvas Block 2.
  3. **Elicit the visual metaphor from Diego** rather than giving him the definition first:
     - *"What do you do with a hot iron on a wrinkled shirt? You make it flat. So if we **iron out** bugs before Friday, what are we doing?"*
     - *"In debugging, if there are 10 possible causes for an outage and you **rule out** the network and **narrow it down** to 2 microservices, what happened?"*
     - *"If we **spin up** a test environment and **kick off** the sprint, are we starting or stopping?"*

---

### 20–28 min | Step 3: Rapid Decode & "Pronoun Sandwich" Drill
*Paste **Canvas Block 3**.*
- **Part A — Upgrade the Formal Spanish-Style Sentence (5 mins):**
  Diego reads the stiff "textbook/Latin" sentence and upgrades it to natural spoken engineering English using Canvas Block 2:
  - **Answer Key:**
    1. *postpone* → **"Let's put off the migration until Monday."** (or *put the migration off*)
    2. *eliminate* → **"We ruled out a database bottleneck."**
    3. *launch/start* → **"Can you spin up a staging server?"**
    4. *split into smaller tasks* → **"Let's break down this epic into smaller tickets."**
    5. *reduce* → **"We narrowed down the bug to the billing service."**
    6. *approve* → **"Wait until the Tech Lead signs off on the release."**
- **Part B — The Pronoun Sandwich Drill (3 mins):**
  Teach the rule (`roll it out` ✅ vs `roll out it` ❌). Say the noun version; Diego fires back the pronoun version at speed:
  - Teacher: *"Roll out the fix."* → Diego: ***"Roll it out."***
  - Teacher: *"Iron out the bugs."* → Diego: ***"Iron them out."***
  - Teacher: *"Put off the meeting."* → Diego: ***"Put it off."***
  - Teacher: *"Spin up the containers."* → Diego: ***"Spin them up."***
  - Teacher: *"Sign off on the release."* (Trap!) → Diego: ***"Sign off on it."*** (3 words = stays at the end!).

### 28–44 min | Step 4: Live Role-Play — The Amsterdam Billing Outage Post-Mortem
*Paste **Canvas Block 4**.*
*(This completes the role-play you didn't get to finish on Nov 13, now supercharged with the phrasal verbs!)*
- **Scenario:** A missing dead-letter queue retry during a legacy billing migration caused 45 minutes of failed invoices and **customer churn** risk.
- **Challenge:** Diego must use **at least 5 phrasal verbs** across the two conversations (tick them off live in chat as he uses them!).
- **Round 1 — Dutch Engineering Team / Polder Model (8 mins):**
  - *Lewis plays Bram (blunt Dutch senior dev):* *"Look, let's not put this off—let's just spin up a hotfix this Saturday and roll it out!"*
  - *Diego's mission:*
    1. **Kick off** the post-mortem & **open up the floor** to Sanne (the quiet junior dev).
    2. Explain how the team **ruled out** a database crash and **narrowed down** the issue to the dead-letter queue.
    3. Push back diplomatically on Saturday work: explain why nobody should **sign off on** a rushed weekend patch until we **iron out** the alerting and **break down** the fix properly on Monday.
- **Round 2 — Non-Technical Product Manager (8 mins):**
  - *Lewis plays the anxious PM:* *"Diego, I have a board call in 10 minutes! Did we lose customers? What happened and when is it fixed?"*
  - *Diego's mission:* Deliver a calm **60-second plain-English update** (no deep jargon), explaining how you'll **iron out** the alerts and **roll out** the fix gradually on Monday to protect **retention**.
