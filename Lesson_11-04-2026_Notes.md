## 2. Key Vocabulary & Expressions

| Term / Idiom | Meaning & Workplace Context |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Crunch time** | A high-pressure period near a deadline when everyone has to work hard |
| **Grueling** *(adj.)* | Extremely tiring, difficult, and demanding |
| **Polder model** | Dutch consensus-based decision-making — extensive consultation where the whole team has a voice (e.g., Guerrilla Games pitching & voting on *Horizon Zero Dawn*) |
| **Radical directness** | Speaking plainly and honestly without sugarcoating |
| **Strict work-life boundaries** | Firmly stopping work at 5:00 PM (*"It's closing time, we'll pick this up tomorrow"*) |
| **Dutch courage** | Bravery (originally a British idiom) |
| **Punctual** *(adj.)* vs. **Overtime** *(n.)* | Being on time vs. working beyond scheduled hours |
| *"To be early is to be on time; to be on time is to be late."* | Classic UK workplace saying about strict punctuality |
| **Customer churn** vs. **Retention** | Percentage of customers who stop using a service (*churn*) vs. keeping them (*retention*) |

## 3. Role-Play Scenario: Senior Tech Lead in Amsterdam (*Marcus*)
**Scenario:** Migrating a legacy billing pipeline to an event-driven microservices architecture with a 6-week MVP deadline for an investor showcase.
- **What went wrong:** Spending the weekend drafting an architecture alone (cutting distributed tracing, DLQ retries, and telemetry) and presenting it on Monday morning.
- **Why the Dutch team pushed back:**
  1. **No consensus (Polder model):** They were left out of the design decision.
  2. **5:00 PM boundary:** Leaving on time at the end of the day is normal — not a sign of disengagement.
- **Better approach practiced:**
  - **Friday 4:00 PM:** Flag the 6-week showcase to the team and propose collaborating on Monday morning rather than working solo over the weekend.
  - **Monday morning:** Open the floor with the **macro problem** first, invite everyone's technical input, and align on scope/trade-offs together.
