## 📦 STAGE 1: Warm-Up — "The Mystery Box" (03:43 – 16:30)

### 🎯 Pedagogical Objective
Lower speaking anxiety, activate the language cortex, and drill spontaneous **auxiliary question inversion** (*"Is it...", "Does it...", "Can you..."*) under deductive game conditions.

### 🕵️‍♂️ The Detective Setup & Game Flow
Louie hid a secret item or location in his mind. Diego had **10 lives (10 questions)** to solve the mystery. Louie could only answer **YES** or **NO**.

* **Round 1 (The Hanoi Pet Market Bird):**
  - Diego's deductive path: *"Is it bigger than a car?"* (No) $\rightarrow$ *"Is it alive?"* (Yes) $\rightarrow$ *"Is it a mammal?"* (No) $\rightarrow$ *"Is it a reptile?"* (No) $\rightarrow$ *"Can it fly?"* (Yes) $\rightarrow$ *"Can you buy it in the street in Vietnam?"* (Yes — a songbird/canary in a traditional Hanoi market!).
* **Round 2 (Everyday Object — Sunglasses):**
  - Diego's deductive path: *"Is it made of plastic?"* (Yes) $\rightarrow$ *"Do people wear it on their face?"* (Yes) $\rightarrow$ *"Does it protect eyes from the sun?"* (Yes — Sunglasses!).
* **Tech Discussion:** Downforce and vacuum suction in electric supercars (Tesla Roadster 0-60 acceleration, aerodynamic grip).

### 🧠 Grammatical Takeaway
When learners feel anxious, they frequently use Spanish-style declarative word order with rising pitch (*"It is bigger than a phone?"*). The Mystery Box naturally forces **auxiliary inversion** (*"Is it bigger...?"*, *"Does it use...?"*, *"Can you buy...?"*). Focusing 100% on the puzzle eliminates grammar hesitation.

## 🏗️ STAGE 2: Core Grammar — "The Sentence Stretcher" (16:31 – 35:45)

### 🎯 Pedagogical Objective
Break the "short answer habit" (answering with 2–4 words) by training the brain to construct **20–30 word multi-clause complex sentences** using Louie's 4-layer scaffolding formula:

$$\text{Seed} \longrightarrow \text{Adjectives} \longrightarrow \text{Prepositional Location} \longrightarrow \text{Causal Clause (Because / Due to)} \longrightarrow \text{Narrative Time Clause (While / Until)}$$

### 🔹 The 4 Rounds of Sentence Stretching

#### Round 1: Everyday Action (The Robot Box)
* **Seed:** *"The robot opened the box."* (5 words)
* **Step 1 (Adjectives & Adverbial detail):** *"The curious silver robot carefully opened the cardboard box."*
* **Step 2 (Location):** *"inside the high security fence..."*
* **Step 3 (Purpose/Cause):** *"...in order to retrieve a golden pencil."*
* **Step 4 (Adjective Order Rule):** Louie corrected adjective sequence: in English, **size always precedes color** (*"the large green tree"*, not *"the green large tree"*).

#### Round 2: Zoo Runaway Bus
* **Seed:** *"The monkey drove the bus."*
* **Step-by-step expansion:** *"The mischievous brown monkey was driving the large blue bus through the crowded zoo while the terrified tourists were running away because the reckless driver had lost the ignition key."* (31 words!)

#### Round 3: Diego's Real-Life Tech Scenario (The DJ Laptop Blue Screen)
* **Diego's Spoken Context:** Explaining why his computer crashed during a high-stakes music performance.
* **Expanded Narrative:** *"My overloaded laptop unexpectedly crashed and suffered a fatal blue screen right before my live DJ set because I was running Rekordbox with high audio buffering while several heavy background applications were multitasking simultaneously."* (35 words!)
* **Tech Dialogue:** Louie and Diego compared Apple Silicon (M-series) unified memory and motherboards vs Windows latency, audio drivers, and CPU thermal throttling; Louie shared his own high-stress gaming in *Tekken*.

#### Round 4: The Elon Musk Ocean Space Rescue

### 6 Spoken Phrasing Polishes
1. **Material Prepositions (`04:20`):**
   * *Spoken:* "Is it made out from plastic?"
   * *Polished:* **"Is it made of plastic?"**
   * *Grammar Rule:* Use *made of* when material doesn't chemically change; *made from* when transformed (e.g. paper from wood).
2. **Adjective Sequence (`22:07`):**
   * *Spoken:* "The bus is green and large."
   * *Polished:* **"The large green bus..."**
   * *Grammar Rule:* English adjective order strictly dictates: $\text{Opinion} \rightarrow \text{Size} \rightarrow \text{Age} \rightarrow \text{Shape} \rightarrow \text{Color}$.
3. **Professional Hardware Terminology (`25:41`):**
   * *Spoken:* "My laptop was upset because doing too much things."
   * *Polished:* **"My laptop froze / crashed because it was handling too many background tasks simultaneously."**
   * *Grammar Rule:* Personifying machines is endearing, but *froze/crashed* and *too many tasks* (countable) represents executive English.
4. **Past Continuous Narrative (`26:45`):**
   * *Spoken:* "The monkey driving the bus in the zoo."
   * *Polished:* **"The monkey was driving the bus through the zoo while the tourists were running away..."**
   * *Grammar Rule:* Anchor background continuous action with *was/were + -ing*.
5. **Causal Connectors (`34:10`):**
   * *Spoken:* "The spaceship crashed because the speed."
   * *Polished:* **"The spaceship crashed due to extreme speed / because of its extreme speed."**
   * *Grammar Rule:* *Because* requires a full clause (*subject + verb*); *due to / because of* takes a noun phrase.
6. **Subject-Verb Agreement (`01:32:30`):**
   * *Spoken:* "The words is difficult."
   * *Polished:* **"The words are difficult / The word module is challenging."**
   * *Grammar Rule:* Plural subject (*words*) requires plural verb (*are*).
